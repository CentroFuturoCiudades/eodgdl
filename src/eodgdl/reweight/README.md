# Reweighting inputs for TMG.SurveyReweight

The IMEPLAN Guadalajara EOD 2023 ships three nested expansion factors (dwelling, person,
trip). This set is what [TMG.SurveyReweight](https://github.com/TravelModellingGroup/TMG.SurveyReweight)
needs to compute **one weight per household** instead: integer-keyed record tables, a zone
system with its aggregation maps, and constraint targets from the 2020 census (summed over
the census units each survey zone is assigned) and from the two external controls the
survey itself used.

Every household starts at weight 1.0 inside the tool; the `ExpansionFactor` columns here
are the survey's own weights, kept for comparison, and the tool ignores them unless a
constraint names them.

Generated by `eodgdl reweight build`. Every definition below is in
`src/eodgdl/reweight/spec.yaml`, which is the thing to edit.

## Files

| `ZoneLabels.csv` | 90 | What each TAZ is: `centralidad`, `municipio`, `CVE_MUN`, sampled dwellings. Not read by the tool. |
| `SampledAGEBs.csv` | 1,046 | Every AGEB the survey sampled (1,019 urban, 27 rural): `Municipality` (CVE_MUN), `centralidad` and `Zone`, `TAZ` (zone × municipality), sampled dwellings, and three targets for 2020 and 2023: `Persons6plus` (residents aged **6 and over**, the tool's `Persons` target, since the survey interviews ages 6+), `Occupants` (residents of private dwellings, **all ages**, the `Occupants` target) and `Dwellings` (private inhabited dwellings). They are built exactly as the zone targets (row corrections, 2023 growth), so they are the AGEB's share of its zone's. A rural AGEB is not a census unit: its values sum the localities inside it that the zone system places in the zone the survey coded (`localities` counts them). Not read by the tool. |

Not read by the tool. |

Use one constraint set (2020 or 2023) at a time. The records are the same for both.

### Ids and zones

- `HouseholdID` = `folio_vivienda`; `PersonID` = `folio_vivienda × 10 + folio_habitante`
  (`folio_habitante` ≤ 9); `TripID` = `folio_viaje`.
- The survey is stratified by *centralidad* (64 zones observed of 71), but 22 of those zones
  straddle two or three municipalities, so neither is an aggregate of the other. The TAZ is
  therefore the **zone × municipality cell**: `TAZ = zone_index × 1000 + CVE_MUN`, where
  `zone_index` is the 1-based position of the centralidad code in the survey's ordered list
  (zone `05` → 5, `Aeropuerto` → 71) and `CVE_MUN` the INEGI municipality key (Guadalajara
  39, Zapopan 120, Tlaquepaque 98, Tlajomulco 97, Tonalá 101, El Salto 70, Juanacatlán 51,
  Ixtlahuacán 44, Zapotlanejo 124). `Zone` and `Municipality` are those two numbers;
  `Region` is 1 everywhere.
- No constraint targets the TAZ itself: seven cells hold fewer than 30 sampled dwellings.
  Zone constraints see 155–417 dwellings and 468+ persons per zone.

## Configuring the XTMF2 model system

```text
SurveyReweight
|- ZoneSystem: LoadZoneSystem
|  |- TAZ            LoadCategoriesFromCSV            ZoneSystem.csv  (column 0)
|  `- SuperZones     LoadCategoryMapFromThirdNormalizedCSV, three of them, all on ZoneSystem.csv:
|                      zone:         Base Column 0, Destination Column 1
|                      municipality: Base Column 0, Destination Column 2
|                      region:       Base Column 0, Destination Column 3
|- SurveyRecords: LoadSurveyRecords   (the loader's default key columns fit these files)
|  |- HouseholdFile  HouseholdRecords.csv   HouseholdIDColumn 0, HouseholdTAZColumn 1
|  |- PersonFile     PersonRecords.csv      PersonIDColumn 0,   PersonHouseholdColumn 1
|  `- TripFile       TripRecords.csv        TripHouseholdColumn 0, TripPersonColumn 1
|- Constraints: LoadConstraints
|  |- HouseholdConstraints   one LoadConstraint per row of Constraints/constraints.csv with table = households
|  |- PersonConstraints      ... table = persons
|  `- TripConstraints        ... table = trips
|- MaximumIterations, Damping, MinimumExpansionFactor, MaximumExpansionFactor
|- UpdatedExpansionFactorsFile
`- ConstraintReportFile
```

For each `LoadConstraint`, read its row in `Constraints/constraints.csv`:

| parameter | value |
|---|---|
| `File` | `Constraints/<file>` |
| `GeographyColumn` | 0 (always the first column) |
| `TargetColumn` | `column_index` |
| `MatchingAttributes` | `matching_attributes`, split on `;` (e.g. `Male;Age18_24` is the two attributes `Male` and `Age18_24`, whose product the tool sums) |
| `CategoryMap` | the super-zone map named by `map_column` (`Zone`, `Municipality` or `Region`) |
| `Constraint Name` | `target_column` |

Parameters worth setting deliberately:

- **`MaximumExpansionFactor`**: the default 50 is far below this survey. The dwelling design
  weights run 2–267 (mean 82), so the tool cannot reach the targets unless the ceiling is
  raised (500 is a safe start; the constraint report will show if it binds).
- **`MinimumExpansionFactor`**: 1 is fine; a household never stands for less than itself.
- **`Damping`**: with 31 constraints per set, several on the same persons, a value below 1
  (0.5–0.8) usually converges more smoothly than 1.
- `Tolerance` per constraint: 0.01 is the tool's default; the region-level `BusBoardings`
  may deserve a looser one (see caveats).

## Attributes

Household (`viv`): `Dwellings` = 1 · `Occupants` = `personas_en_vivienda` (all ages, top-coded 10) ·
`HasCar`, `HasMoto`, `HasBike` = at least one of `n_autos_camionetas`, `n_motos`, `n_bicicletas` ·
`HasInternet` = `tiene_internet`.

Person (`hab`, ages 6+ only): `Persons` = 1 · `Male`/`Female` from `sexo_nacimiento` ·
`Age6_11 … Age60p` from `edad` at the census cut points (6–11, 12–14, 15–17, 18–24, 25–59,
60+) · `Employed`/`Unemployed`/`Inactive`/`NotEmployed` from `trabajo_semana_pasada`, ages
12+ only (`Employed` = Tiempo completo, Medio tiempo, Tenía trabajo pero no trabajó;
`Unemployed` = Busco trabajo; `Inactive` = the other five answers; `NotEmployed` = everyone
12+ who is not `Employed`, the 4,370 with no answer included) · `Cyclist` = at least one
bicycle leg (Bicicleta, Mi Bici) on a trip whose motive is Trabajar or Estudiar.

Trip (`trips` as `load_eod` returns them, without the rows it marks as non-trips):
`Trips` = 1 · boardings = the number of the trip's legs by `Bus` (CAMIÓN O AUTOBÚS), `Rail`
(TREN LIGERO), `BRT` (MI MACRO), `Sitren` (lines 1–3), `OtherTransit` (Servicio suburbano,
Taxi colectivo, Transporte informal), and their sum `TransitBoardings` · `BicycleTrip` = any
bicycle leg.

## Targets

Census values come from INEGI's Censo 2020 through `mxcensus` (`load_census(state=14)`,
the AGEB and rural-locality frames). Which rows make up each survey zone is decided by
`zoning.assign_units` from the centralidad polygons the survey was fielded on and the zones
the survey coded for its dwellings and trip ends (the survey's zone is a function of the
AGEB code: each of the 1,693 codes it records has one zone across dwellings, origins and
destinations; 1,694 before the hand revisions moved one AGEB's only trip end home); IMEPLAN's AGEB table plays no part. An urban AGEB is never split: a sampled one takes
the zone coded for its dwellings (26 of them against the polygons, La Aurora in Juanacatlán
among them), one with trip ends but no dwelling the zone coded most for them (15 against the
polygons, the largest a campus coded `68B` whose residents mostly live in `44`'s polygon),
any other goes whole to the polygon holding most of its population by census blocks, and
one lying mostly outside every polygon to the polygon holding the most of the rest, or the
nearest one: every urban AGEB is in a zone. The census counts rural population by
locality only, so a rural AGEB the survey records (dwellings or trip ends) goes whole to the zone it coded,
with every locality of it inside some polygon, the survey's frame, wherever the polygons would
put it, so its sampled dwellings expand into all of its in-frame population; its localities
outside every polygon stay out of the targets (IMEPLAN planned a sample in almost none of
them), except that a rural AGEB with none inside a polygon keeps its locality nearest to the
zone's polygon, and a sampled one whose counted localities hold fewer dwellings than it
sampled adds its nearest other localities until they can (Cuexcomatitlán for 51F); a rural AGEB the survey never records leaves its localities
with the polygon containing them. Two rural AGEBs with trip ends hold no census locality and
map to their zone with no population. 2,039 AGEBs and 269 localities are in the universe (`ZoneAssignment.csv`): every
one of the survey's 304,882 trip ends that is not at an access point lies in the zone its
AGEB is assigned, and the build fails otherwise (`zoning.check_assignment`). Suppressed census cells stay blank. Columns are summed by
zone or municipality, then combined:

| target | census |
|---|---|
| `Dwellings` | `TVIVPARHAB` — private inhabited dwellings, 1,462,391 |
| `Occupants` | `OCUPVIVPAR`, as published — it already covers every private inhabited dwelling (it equals `POBTOT − POBCOL` exactly), so it is **not** rescaled |
| `HasCar`, `HasMoto`, `HasBike`, `HasInternet` | `VPH_AUTOM`, `VPH_MOTO`, `VPH_BICI`, `VPH_INTER`, lifted row by row to all private inhabited dwellings (see the corrections below the table) |
| `Persons` | `P_6A11 + P_12A14 + P_15A17 + P_18YMAS` — ages 6+, so the age bands partition it; 4,680,094 after the row corrections below (4,659,645 with a stated age as published) |
| `Male`, `Female` | the `_M` / `_F` versions of the same |
| `Age6_11 … Age60p` | `P_6A11`, `P_12A14`, `P_15A17`, `P_18A24`, `P_18YMAS − P_18A24 − P_60YMAS`, `P_60YMAS`, and their `_M` / `_F` versions for the 12 sex × age crosses |
| `Employed` (zone) | `POCUPADA` — the occupied population, 12+ |
| `NotEmployed` (zone) | `P_12A14 + P_15A17 + P_18YMAS − POCUPADA` — everyone else aged 12+: unemployed, inactive, activity not specified. `PE_INAC` is not a target on its own: the survey's non-respondents would be forced to match the census's 16,000 unknowns |
| `Cyclist` (municipality) | Census 2020 extended questionnaire (via `mxcensus`): persons whose mode to work or to school is a bicycle, weighted; **101,907** over the nine whole municipalities, the INEGI figure IMEPLAN calibrated its person weights to. The microdata has no AGEB, so each municipality's count is scaled by the universe's share of its population (`coverage.csv`), which gives **100,870** |
| `BusBoardings` (region) | **1,851,750**, SETRAN's 2023 daily bus boardings, the control of the survey's trip weight |

**Two row-level corrections** (`row_scale` in `spec.yaml`, `targets.person_scale` /
`targets.dwelling_scale`), applied to each census row before summing:

- **Person targets** (`row_scale: persons`) are multiplied by
  `(POBTOT − POBCOL) / residents with a stated age`. The census counts residents of
  collective quarters (`POBCOL`), whom a household survey never samples — a third of
  zone `45`, Puente Grande's prisons — and in a few AGEBs
  INEGI left the occupants of dwellings without occupant information without an age: they
  are in `POBTOT` but in no band, sex or activity (17.5 % of `63F`'s residents, 12–13 % of
  `16` and `20`, 0.9 % of the universe).
  The factor restricts the row to private dwellings and spreads the unknown ages over
  the known bands in proportion. A row with residents but no stated age at all (117 rows,
  2,525 residents, mostly tiny units whose age cells INEGI suppressed) takes its zone's
  mix instead (`targets.fill_unaged`): each person count per private-dwelling resident of
  the zone's rows that state ages, times the row's own. The census publishes neither group's composition, so
  both are assumed to share the row's known mix.
- **`VPH_*` targets** (`row_scale: dwellings`) are counted over `VIVPARH_CV`, which
  INEGI defines as *including* the dwellings without occupant information and leaving out
  only mobile homes, shelters and non-residential premises (0.09 % fewer than
  `TVIVPARHAB`). Where INEGI left those dwellings' characteristics blank they sit in
  `VIVPARH_CV` with no attribute; they are estimated from the same signal — the row's
  residents without an age over its occupants per dwelling, capped at its dwellings
  without occupant information — and the count is divided by the dwellings that remain,
  then lifted to `TVIVPARHAB`. `VIVPAR_HAB` (7 % fewer) is **not** their base: an
  earlier version divided by it and put `HasInternet` above `Dwellings` in five zones.

With both, persons 6+ are 85–96 % of occupants in every zone (the survey: ~93 %; the
uncorrected census: 35–96 %). `Occupants` needs neither (`OCUPVIVPAR` already counts the
occupants of every private dwelling: it equals `POBTOT − POBCOL` exactly), nor does
`Cyclist` (the extended questionnaire samples private dwellings).

**The universe is not the nine whole municipalities.** Guadalajara and Tlaquepaque are
inside it whole, Zapopan, El Salto, Tlajomulco and Tonalá to 99 %, Juanacatlán 86 %,
Ixtlahuacán 79 %, Zapotlanejo 72 % (`coverage.csv`). Every target here is a sum over the
universe; a whole-municipality figure from any other source must be scaled the same way
before it becomes a target.

**2023 set.** Each AGEB's census values are multiplied by the CONAPO 2023/2020 population
ratio of its municipality, pooled over the 5-year bands the target spans and over the sex it
names (6–11 → 5–14; 12–14 → 10–14; 15–17 → 15–19; 18–24 → 15–24; 25–59, 60+ as they are;
cyclists → 5+; economic activity → 10+; dwellings → all ages), then summed. It is a municipal
growth factor, not a projection of each zone. Targets that partition the same people in the
census are kept partitioning them, because each band grows at its own rate: a sex × age
cross grows by its band and sex; an age band grows, row by row, at the combined rate of its
two crosses (`grow_with`); `Persons`, `Male` and `Female` are the sums of their grown bands
(`sum_of`); and `Employed` and `NotEmployed`, grown with the 10+ population, are rescaled
together to the grown 12+ bands (`grow_within`), which keeps 2020's employment rate. So the
bands still sum to `Persons`, each sex's bands to its total and the two activity targets to
the 12+ population exactly, and a band exceeds its two crosses only by the cells INEGI
suppressed, as in 2020. Study-area totals go from 1,462,391 to 1,505,698 dwellings and
4,680,094 to 4,850,841 persons 6+.

**Motorcycles grow with the fleet, not the population.** `HasMoto` is scaled instead by
the 2023/2020 ratio of *registered private motorcycles* in its municipality (INEGI's VMRC,
`data/VMRC_AMG.csv`, `vmrc:` in `spec.yaml`): +33 % (Guadalajara) to +64 % (Tlajomulco),
+44 % over the nine municipalities, against +3 % of population. The survey's motorcycle
share had sat 1.4× above the 2020 census rate in the median zone; the fleet-scaled
target moves it from 173,124 to 254,947, and the survey at its design weight to 0.95× of
it. The fleet counts vehicles, including firms', so this is a proxy for the growth of
dwellings with a motorcycle. Cars are left on population growth: their fleet grew 8 %,
but the survey already matched the population-scaled car target (1.001×) and would fall
to 0.94× of a fleet-scaled one — cars grew per owning dwelling more than in owning
dwellings.

**Internet grows with ENDUTIH.** `HasInternet` keeps its population growth and is also
multiplied by INEGI ENDUTIH's 2020–2023 growth in the share of dwellings with internet
(`rates.endutih_internet` in `spec.yaml`, `data/ENDUTIH_internet_AMG.csv`, derived by
`scripts/endutih_internet.py`): 0.745 → 0.890 in Jalisco's localities of 100,000+ (the
public files have no city identifier), ×1.193 ± 0.028. Only the *change* is borrowed, so
any wording difference between ENDUTIH and the census cancels; each census row is capped
at its dwellings (two zones reach it), so the study-area target grows ×1.196, to
1,220,920. Unlike motorcycles, this moves the target *past* the survey: at its design
weights the survey reaches 0.95× of it (1.13× before). Either the survey under-reports
internet or ENDUTIH's domain grew faster than the metro; the data here cannot tell which.

## Caveats

- **Sparse cells.** The sex × age crosses have as few as 3 records in a zone
  (`Male_Age12_14`). Every target with a positive value has at least one matching record
  (checked by `eodgdl reweight check`), but those households will carry large corrections.
  Drop the crosses if the weight range or the constraint report says so.
- **No unemployment control.** INEGI's unemployed (`PDESOCUP`, 40,565 in the universe)
  are the people aged 12+ without a job who looked for one in the reference week; anyone
  without work who did not look is inactive. The survey never asks about job search. Its
  `Busco trabajo` answer (122 persons, 13,704 at the design weight) is the closest match and
  gives a third of the census; its `ocupacion == Desempleado` answer (790 persons, 80,335)
  gives double, and only 102 people gave both. With the two proxies bracketing the target
  by a factor of three either way, no reliable target exists, so it was left out.
  `Unemployed` (the `Busco trabajo` reading) stays in the person records.
- **Bus boardings.** One household weight cannot scale a household's bus legs without
  scaling its car trips, and bus riders skew low-income, so matching SETRAN through the
  weight distorts the other margins (`reports/expansion_factors.qmd`, §6). It is here
  because it was asked for; treat it as optional and watch what it does to `HasCar`.
- **No income control.** The census has no income variable and 58 % of households did
  not report theirs; income is neither an attribute nor a target.
- **4,370 persons** gave no activity answer. They are 0 in `Employed`, `Unemployed` and
  `Inactive` and 1 in `NotEmployed`, so the two targets in use partition the 12+ population
  and nobody is left in a residual the census does not have.
- **Under-6s** are absent from the person records (the survey interviews 6+); `Occupants`
  counts them on the household side.
- **Cyclist** uses the census commute definition; IMEPLAN's own reweighted set was narrower
  and cannot be reproduced from the shipped columns.
- **Suppression.** INEGI blanks cells of one or two units. Sums skip them, so the sexed
  targets fall short of the unsexed ones by up to 41 persons in a zone (469 in all, after
  the row corrections).

## Regenerating

Needs the `reweight` extra (`uv sync --extra reweight`), which brings `mxcensus`; the
census files are fetched once and cached.

```bash
uv run eodgdl reweight build --data data --out output/reweight   # build, write, check
uv run eodgdl reweight check output/reweight                     # check a set again
```

`check` reads the files back the way the tool's loaders do (integer keys, unique ids, joins,
every TAZ mapped, every map geography present in every constraint file, every positive
target with at least one matching record) and lists what would fail.
